## 5. First executive synthesis — QQQ-JS-ST-01
**Evidence status:** initial synthesis grounded in the author's public description and the published contents map; full chapter-by-chapter verification remains IN PROGRESS.

### Three core learning ideas
1. **Look beyond immediate tasks.** Strategic thought widens the frame from the urgent symptom to the situation, its context, forces, constraints and plausible future changes. It does not guarantee that the future can be predicted with certainty.
2. **Inspect the mental model.** Decisions depend on how the problem is framed and what assumptions are accepted. Use explicit questions, counter-examples and alternative explanations before committing to a strategy.
3. **Connect direction to execution.** Strategy must be grounded in internal capabilities and external realities, then translated into coherent vision, mission, values, objectives and policies. For QQQ, “ends / ways / means” is an additional application heuristic used to test whether the intended outcome, proposed approach and available resources fit together; do not attribute this exact formula to the author without a source reference.

### QQQ decision canvas
Before writing a plan, answer:
1. **Situation:** What is happening, where and when? Which claims are verified?
2. **Core challenge:** What is the underlying decision problem, not merely the visible symptom?
3. **Purpose and values:** What outcome matters, and what constraints are non-negotiable?
4. **External context:** Which actors, rules, technologies, economic/social forces and trends shape the situation?
5. **Internal capability:** What resources, skills, data, time and permissions are available or missing?
6. **Strategic choices:** What options exist, what will deliberately not be done, and why?
7. **Scenarios:** What baseline, disruption and alternative outcomes deserve review?
8. **Action:** What bounded first step, owner, date, dependencies and rollback are appropriate?
9. **Evidence:** What observation would show progress—or falsify the plan?
10. **Learning:** When will the decision be reviewed and adapted?

### Application example — safe QQQ integration
**Question:** Should QQQ connect a new AI provider directly to production?
- Verified starting point: design docs and a local dashboard prototype exist; a live provider connection is not verified.
- Strategic framing: assess use-case value, data class, model fit, privacy, reliability, cost, latency, dependencies and exit path.
- First choice: use synthetic data and an isolated, read-only pilot rather than immediate production integration.
- Acceptance: exact provider/model ID, repeatable tests, security/privacy review, measured cost/latency, failure record and rollback plan.
- Stop condition: missing authorization, sensitive-data risk, unbounded access, failed security gate or incomplete evidence.

This is an original QQQ application exercise; it is not presented as an example quoted from the book.

## 6. DIIP study pipeline
`SOURCE → DATA → INFORMATION → INTELLIGENCE → IMPACT PROPOSAL → REVIEW`

- **Data:** bibliographic record, book sections, programme metadata and source dates.
- **Information:** concept map, definitions, arguments, relationships, assumptions and counterpoints.
- **Intelligence:** source-backed insight relevant to a specified decision; competing interpretations remain visible.
- **Impact:** a measured pilot or explicitly unmeasured proposal with owner, baseline, acceptance criteria and review date.

Do not claim a 120-hour disaster forecast, 100% accuracy, zero waste or microsecond understanding based on strategic-thinking concepts alone.
